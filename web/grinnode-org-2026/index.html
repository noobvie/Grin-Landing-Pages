01 / Why run a node

A node is how you verify Grin for yourself.

Wallets are useful, but reachable full nodes are the network's public backbone. They validate chain history, relay blocks and transactions, and give new users more peers to connect to.

✔️

Verify independently

Your node checks chain rules directly instead of trusting another server's view of the network.

🌐

Accept inbound peers

A reachable Grin node helps other nodes discover, sync, and stay connected to the chain.

🛡️

Improve resilience

More independent operators means fewer fragile points and a healthier privacy-focused network.

02 / Operator route

Pick the path that matches your setup.

Linux for always-on public infrastructure, Docker or Umbrel for home servers and Raspberry Pi, or any desktop OS for a full-featured wallet with Grim — three paths, one network.

Linux server path

Best for VPS operators, public services, snapshots, monitoring, and community infrastructure.

  • 1
    Clone Grin Node Toolkit and run the interactive Bash menu.
  • 2
    Build or install a node, then bootstrap chain data from a trusted snapshot.
  • 3
    Publish API, file server, explorer, stats, mining, or wallet services as needed.

Docker / Umbrel

Best for home servers, Raspberry Pi, NAS, and anyone who wants container-based deployment without a full VPS.

  • 1
    Install Docker on your device, or set up Umbrel on a home server or Raspberry Pi.
  • 2
    Install Grin via the Umbrel community app store or use Docker Compose to pull the image.
  • 3
    Node starts, syncs the chain, and runs fully containerised with no manual binary management.

Desktop path (any OS)

Best for people who want no VPS, a familiar GUI, and a full wallet with Tor-backed slatepack flows.

  • 1
    Download Grim wallet from gri.mw — Windows, macOS, and Linux.
  • 2
    Create or restore a wallet. Grim connects to the Grin network automatically.
  • 3
    Send and receive via Slatepack, explore history, manage multiple wallets.

06 / Why Grin

New to crypto? Here is what makes Grin different.

Nine plain-language reasons — no jargon, no hype, just the things that make Grin a genuinely new idea.

⏱
The first money where nobody decided how much to make
Pick any point in the last hundred years and ask: who decided how much new money was created that year? The answer is always a person or institution — a central bank setting rates, a mining company capping gold output, a developer choosing 21 million as Bitcoin’s limit. Grin is the first monetary system where the correct answer is nobody. The emission rate — 60 GRIN per minute — is not a policy choice. It is a mathematical side-effect of mining one block every 60 seconds. Nobody tuned it to attract investors. Nobody can vote to change it. No committee can pause it. Time passes, blocks get mined, coins appear. The rule is enforced by every node on the network, verified by anyone who runs one, and has no off switch.
👁️
Your balance is invisible — to everyone
On Bitcoin, anyone can paste an address into a website and read its full balance and history. On Grin, amounts are hidden inside the math of each transaction. There are no reusable addresses, and spent records are deleted from the chain. Nobody can look up what you hold or trace who paid you — not an explorer, not a data broker, not a government request.
🔒
You can’t accidentally send a public transaction
Some privacy coins have a “shielded” mode users have to remember to enable — and most forget. Grin has no transparent mode at all. Every transaction, whether you are a developer testing on testnet or a person paying a friend, uses the same privacy-preserving math. Private is not a checkbox. It is the only way Grin works.
🗑️
The blockchain trims its own history
When you spend a Grin coin, the old record is cut from the chain automatically — a technique called cut-through. Bitcoin’s full history weighs over 600 GB and grows forever. Grin’s full chain is around 5 GB after seven years. A cheap VPS (around $20/year) or a home computer can run a full node. You do not need a data center to verify your own transactions.
⏰
60 coins per minute — no halving, no cliff
Many cryptocurrencies halve the mining reward every few years, creating scheduled shocks and speculation cycles. Grin pays exactly 60 GRIN every 60 seconds, forever. The rule is in the code, not controlled by a foundation or a vote. Miners who secure the network are always compensated, and the supply curve has no surprises baked in.
💬
Send to someone who is offline
Grin uses Slatepack: a block of text you exchange to complete a transaction. Paste it in a chat message, email it, copy it to a USB drive. Neither party needs to be online at the same time, and there is no public address to look up or share. It works like passing a signed note — only the two of you know what it says.
📉
Inflation shrinks every year — automatically
Because a fixed 60 GRIN per minute is added to a growing total supply, the percentage inflation rate falls every year on its own — no committee vote, no scheduled upgrade. Pure division. By the time someone discovers Grin decades from now, inflation will be a small fraction of what it was at launch, driven entirely by math and time.
📄
The whole protocol fits in a research paper
MimbleWimble was first described in a nine-page anonymous document. Grin’s core design is compact enough to read over a weekend. No complex tokenomics. No governance tokens. No multi-year roadmap of scheduled forks. An engineer can audit the critical code in days. What you read is what it does — nothing hidden, nothing promised for later.
⏳
Copy the code. You can’t copy the clock.
Anyone can fork Grin’s codebase tomorrow. But a fork starts at block 1 with zero history. Grin has seven years of solved puzzles behind it. That accumulated proof-of-work cannot be duplicated — you cannot re-mine blocks that have already been mined, just as you cannot re-live time that has already passed.
